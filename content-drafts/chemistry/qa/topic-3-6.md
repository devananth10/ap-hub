# Topic 3.6 — Deviation from Ideal Gas Law

Reviewed 2026-10-18. Eleven original questions; **human scientific review and owner approval pending**. Exact ID `topic-3-6`; not imported.

Authority: [College Board CED](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-chemistry-course-and-exam-description.pdf), Fall 2024, accessed 2026-10-17, printed p.65 / PDF72. LO 3.6.A and EK 3.6.A.1: attractions, particularly near condensation, and finite particle volumes, particularly at high pressure; suggested skill 6.E. Ideal-gas/KMT and dispersion are prerequisites. No van der Waals equation, critical constants, fugacity, or phase diagram is required. Pressure ratios are defined in the prompt and interpreted relative to one, rather than requiring a new named gas equation.

Separate prompt/option review after drafting produced A/C/B/D/A/B/C/A/D/B/D, matching all keys. A/B/C/D = 3/3/2/3. Each explanation was checked for sign, fixed variables, and all alternatives. Distractors involving negative molecular volumes, zero high-density pressure, and compression creating matter were replaced with finite-volume sign and unsupported monotonic-departure misconceptions.

| Suffix | Separate science/evidence check |
| --- | --- |
| 001 | Low pressure/high temperature generally favor ideal approximation within a gas phase; not crowding, proximity to condensation, or slow particles unable to attract. |
| 002 | Attraction-dominated measured P below nRT/V fits reduced wall momentum transfer. Finite size does not create extra free space, and attractions do not imply the ideal prediction is lower. |
| 003 | Dominant repulsive finite-volume effects can raise P above ideal at fixed n/T/V. A real gas need not always be below ideal merely because attractions also exist. |
| 004 | Comparable-size nonpolar X with greater polarizability can have stronger dispersion and a larger negative departure in the specified attraction-dominated regime. Nonpolar does not mean attraction-free. |
| 005 | Higher KE reduces relative attraction importance in the given dilute regime; forces/radii do not literally vanish and n does not change. |
| 006 | Dilution reduces occupied-volume fraction and interaction influence, without changing molecular size/count or removing thermal motion. Single gas phase is explicit. |
| 007 | Defined ratios .99/.86/1.20 show near-ideal/negative/positive departures during single-phase compression. Opposing effects can change the sign; neither positivity nor an initial negative value fixes all future behavior. |
| 008 | Near unity at one condition supports accuracy there, not zero individual interactions, zero radius, or all-condition ideality. Partial cancellation is possible. |
| 009 | Macroscopic insert reduces accessible V. Using the empty-vessel V can itself underpredict P even for an ideal gas. Correct inputs before assigning intrinsic nonideality; point particles do not penetrate solid inserts. |
| 010 | Different absolute P with unknown n is insufficient to compare departures. Need each nRT/V baseline; mass/identity/proton count alone do not replace amount information. |
| 011 | With Pmeasured=rPideal, r<1, Mcalc=Mtrue/r>Mtrue. A fixed true molar mass does not validate an estimate based on an inaccurate model. Sign could reverse for a positive pressure departure, which is excluded by the stated conditions. |

The science script separately verifies pressure direction for an accessible-volume error and the molar-mass bias using an independent illustrative input set; it does not pretend these are experimental measurements. Other checks are qualitative and explicitly conditional on the dominant regime. The set varies condition selection, cause inference, competing effects, polarizability comparison, thermal/density trends, hypothetical data, cancellation, calibration, baseline identifiability, and analytical bias rather than assuming all real gases deviate in one direction. No third-party questions consulted. Same-assistant separate checks are not human scientific review.
