| 001 | A small solute with several accessible O–H groups is likely more water-compatible than hexane-compatible through hydrogen bonding. Molecular dissolution need not make ions; identity/mass alone do not fix solvent response. |
| 002 | I2/hexane dispersion compatibility versus water's solvent interactions supports the usual prediction. Water still has dispersion; nonpolar does not mean full ions or permanent dipoles. Not zero aqueous solubility. |
| 003 | One O–H with a growing hydrocarbon region changes the overall interaction balance; same functional-group count does not imply identical solubility. Greater mass alone is not a universal aqueous-solubility rule. |
| 004 | Favorable ion–dipole solvation does not guarantee every ionic solid dissolves completely; the original solid and solvent contacts also matter. Water is neutral but polar, and ions need not become neutral atoms. |
| 005 | NaCl receives much better permanent-dipole solvation in water than in hexane. Do not assert zero induced interactions or generalize to every possible organic ionic salt. Same ion charge does not mean same solvent interaction. |
| 006 | Larger ionic charge can strengthen both hydration and the original lattice. Neither favorable nor unfavorable side alone proves a solubility ranking; similar radius alone is insufficient. |
| 007 | Dissolution of intact molecules replaces some old contacts with solute–solvent contacts. Not decomposition, full separation without cohesion, or mandatory permanent ionization. |
| 008 | A molecular oxygen acceptor can receive water's O–H hydrogen bond without being a donor. No need for a full oxide ion or both partners donating in each contact. Does not guarantee unlimited miscibility. |
| 009 | “Like dissolves like” is a qualitative tendency, not complete polar miscibility, zero nonpolar aqueous solubility, or a same-formula requirement. |
| 010 | Separate bulk phases can still have finite limited mutual solubility. Reliable trace detection does not force reaction, equal layer compositions, or complete miscibility. |
| 011 | Given ethanol cosolvent observation is consistent with water-compatible O–H plus nonpolar-region solute contacts. Does not require ionization, ignore solute contacts, or impose a fixed water:solute particle ratio. |
