| Suffix | Science and alternative-answer check |
| --- | --- |
| 001 | Positive metal cores plus delocalized valence electrons; not alternating metal anions, molecules, or entirely localized valence electrons. Cores are not bare nuclei. |
| 002 | Electronic motion carries current while cores stay near sites; not mobile cations/anions or whole molecules. |
| 003 | Comparable-radius B replaces host sites: substitutional. Not gap occupancy, discrete molecules, or an ionic lattice. |
| 004 | Explicit small-carbon gap occupancy: interstitial. Does not infer one universal Fe:C formula or the phase of every steel. |
| 005 | Given distortions can hinder rearrangement and explain observed resistance. Does not claim every alloy is harder or that every individual interaction strengthens. Easier sliding would contradict the observation; mass alone does not determine hardness. |
| 006 | Cores can shift while attractions to the electron population persist. Not core neutralization, fixed molecular pair bonds, or liquid-like core flow with fixed electrons. |
| 007 | Two stated homogeneous alloys have different 4:1 and 3:2 atom ratios. This does not claim arbitrary ratios of arbitrary metals always form one phase. |
| 008 | Interior and surface Ni support bulk distribution rather than surface-only coating. Composition measurements alone do not locate lattice sites or prove a pure-compound formula. |
| 009 | Explicit simplified +2 cores: 100×2=200 electrons. Other choices leave net +150/+100/−200. No assumption about a universal metal electron contribution. |
| 010 | Site replacement and gap occupancy can coexist in the stated multi-component model; the categories are not mutually exclusive here. |
| 011 | Hardness alone cannot distinguish atomic site occupancy. Neither alloy type is excluded; hardness is not a direct measurement of radius/site position. |
