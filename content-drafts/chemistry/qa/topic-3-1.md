| 001 | CH4 has no net permanent dipole but has dispersion from temporary/induced correlated fluctuations. Does not require ions or pre-existing permanent polarization. Dispersion also exists in polar species; van der Waals is not used synonymously. |

| 004 | Polar dimethyl ether has dispersion/dipole-dipole interactions; an acceptor without a conventional donor does not self hydrogen-bond in the stated AP model. Not full ions. |

| 006 | Na+ favors water Oδ− pointing toward it. Overall water neutrality does not erase the dipole; no water dissociation is required. |
| 007 | Intact HCl can induce an Ar dipole. Ar has no permanent dipole, full ions were not specified, and no conventional hydrogen-bond acceptor is supplied. |
| 008 | Head-to-tail unlike partial charges favor attraction over head-to-head like ends at comparable distances. Magnitude alone is insufficient; neutral molecules still interact. |
| 009 | Large polarizable nonpolar molecules can have stronger total attractions than small polar ones. No blanket hydrogen-bond/dipole/dispersion ranking independent of size and shape. |
| 010 | A donor/acceptor contact can be intramolecular in a biomolecule without covalent connectivity changes. Carbonyl O can accept; one contact does not determine all folding behavior. |
| 011 | At fixed geometry/polarizability, a stronger permanent dipole produces a stronger induced attraction. It does not require permanent molecular ionization or imply arbitrary real substitutions leave all other properties fixed. |
