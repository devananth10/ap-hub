| Suffix | Science/alternative-answer review |
| --- | --- |
| 001 | Ionic layer shifts can bring like charges adjacent, favoring fracture. No ion neutralization, sign reversal without electron transfer, or discrete neutral molecular lattice. |
| 002 | Diamond's three-dimensional covalent network resists rearrangement; I2 is molecular with intermolecular cohesion. Not a universal covalent-hardness rule; graphite illustrates the role of dimensionality. |
| 003 | A continuous Cu path can conduct electronically while NaCl stays crystalline. The whole composite's conductivity does not prove each component conducts; neutral formula units are not charge carriers. The conducting path is explicitly supplied, so no percolation-threshold knowledge is needed. |
| 004 | CO2 sublimation without decomposition overcomes between-molecule attractions, not internal C=O bonds, a nonexistent covalent network, or an ionic lattice. |
| 005 | Higher molecular-solid melting point does not establish stronger intramolecular bonds; packing and intermolecular contacts matter. Total grams are not the missing criterion. |
| 006 | For comparable stable liquids, stronger cohesion generally lowers same-temperature vapor pressure and raises boiling point at the same external pressure. No universal classification from size alone or ionic decomposition. |
| 007 | High melting point plus poor solid-state conduction does not uniquely distinguish ionic and three-dimensional covalent network solids. Additional evidence must be appropriate to the material; melting without decomposition is not assumed universally possible. |
| 008 | Folded shape/function can change with rearranged noncovalent contacts while covalent backbone remains intact. Formula/mass/backbone alone do not fix binding geometry. |
| 009 | Graphite has strong within-sheet bonding and weaker interlayer interactions; sheets can slide without breaking every within-sheet bond. Not isolated atoms or full alternating carbon ions. |
| 010 | Interstitial obstruction can reduce bendability while mobile electrons preserve conduction. Does not assign solid-metal current to moving cores, stationary ionic charges, or localized electrons. |
| 011 | Dense liquid retains neighboring interacting molecules with changing contacts. Not inflated molecular sizes, isolated atoms, fixed crystalline sites, or total elimination of cohesion. |
